Checked in your browser only. Nothing you type is sent or saved.

    What makes a password strong?

    • Length beats complexity. Aim for 14 or more characters. A phrase of four random words is strong and easy to remember.
    • Never reuse passwords. If one site is breached, attackers try the same password everywhere.
    • Avoid personal details. Names, birthdays, phone numbers and "Qona" or "sacco" are the first things guessed.
    • Use a password manager and turn on two-factor authentication wherever it is offered.

    How to spot a fake email

    Six warning signs to check before you click anything

    • 📧

      Check the sender

      Look at the real address, not just the display name. Lookalike domains such as qona-sacco-verify.com are not yours.

    • ⏰

      Urgency and threats

      "Act now or lose access." Attackers rush you so you skip the thinking step.

    • 👋

      Generic greeting

      Real messages from people you deal with usually use your name and details.

    • 🔗

      Hover before you click

      Hover over a link to see where it really goes. Shortened links hide the destination.

    • 📎

      Unexpected attachments

      Be careful with invoices you didn't expect, and files ending in .zip, .exe or .pdf.exe.

    • 🔑

      Asks for secrets or money

      No genuine IT desk asks for your password. Be wary of gift cards and urgent transfers.

    Not sure? Don't click. Contact the sender using a phone number or address you already know, then report it.
